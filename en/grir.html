The most confusing part of MM is not creating documents but what happens in the books while the goods are in and the invoice is not. The answer is a temporary account, GR/IR (goods receipt / invoice receipt clearing): a liability at goods receipt, cleared when the invoice arrives. This page explains the three-way match, GR-based IV, the four tolerances, invoice blocking and release, and the inverted route of invoicing before receipt.

1. What GR/IR is: an account that exists purely to wait for an invoice

Point in timeBusiness stepAccounting entryGR/IR balance
① Goods receiptMIGO 101Stock Dr 216,000.00 / GR/IR Cr 216,000.00Credit 216,000.00 (accrual liability)
② InvoiceMIROGR/IR Dr 216,000.00 / payables Cr 216,000.000 (that order cleared)
③ PaymentF-53Payables Dr 216,000.00 / bank Cr 216,000.000

The GR/IR balance is itself a to-do list: a non-zero balance means either goods received without an invoice (credit), an invoice without goods (debit), or a quantity / amount difference. At month-end finance pulls MB5S and walks it line by line; anything long outstanding has to be accrued or cleared.

It is not a normal payableGR/IR carries no vendor, so you cannot pay it directly and you cannot age it. Its only exit is invoice verification (or manual clearing with MR11). Vendor reconciliation runs through accounts payable (reconciliation account 160000), not through GR/IR.

2. The three-way match and GR-based IV

Invoice verification looks at three documents at once: the purchase order (how much should be paid), the goods receipt (how much actually arrived) and the invoice (how much the vendor wants). On the Invoice tab of the PO item there is a GR-based IV checkbox, and it decides whether an invoice may be posted before the receipt:

GR-based IVBehaviourTypical scenario
Ticked (default)When MIRO references the PO it only proposes the quantity already received; with no receipt no item appears and no invoice can be postedThe vast majority of purchasing: receive first, invoice second
Not tickedWhen MIRO references the PO it proposes the ordered quantity, so the invoice can be posted before the goods arrive (invoice first)Prepayments, service purchasing, vendors who invoice early
The most common reason MIRO "will not open"You enter the purchase order in MIRO and the item list stays empty, with only "no items to be invoiced" — nine times out of ten GR-based IV is ticked and nothing has been received yet. Either post the receipt in MIGO first, or remove the tick as described in the next section. The source material records this verbatim as "this one matters, this is where I had not ticked it".

3. IR before GR: removing GR-based IV

  1. Copy two POs (create them in ME21N with reference to an existing order, or use the copy function), for example 4500014508 and 4500014509.ME21N
  2. Receive neither of them, go straight into MIRO and reference the first one (GR-based IV still ticked) — no item appears. That is the default behaviour.MIRO
  3. Change the info record with ME12 and remove GR-based IV; then use ME22N to untick the same checkbox on the item of the second PO.ME12
  4. Back in MIRO, reference the second PO: this time quantity and amount appear; enter the invoice amount and press ✓ to post directly (an invoice document is created).MIRO
  5. The PO history now has an invoice number but still no receipt — GR/IR carries a debit balance (invoice in, goods missing).ME23N
  6. Now post MIGO 101: stock +, GR/IR credited back, GR/IR returns to zero; the PO history has both lines and still-to-be-delivered drops to 0.MIGO

5. The four tolerances (OMR6): how much difference counts as acceptable

Tolerance keyNameHow the system calculates itWhat happens beyond it
BDForm small differences (rounding)If the absolute invoice balance is within the upper limit it is posted to a small-difference account and the gap is closedAbove the limit the difference cannot be absorbed — error
DQQuantity deviationWith a receipt: net price × (invoice quantity − (delivered − invoiced)); without a receipt: net price × (invoice quantity − (ordered − invoiced))Beyond it: quantity deviation exceeds tolerance
PPPrice changePer line, the difference between invoice quantity × net price and the order net price, compared with the upper and lower limits (amount and percentage)Beyond it: M8083 price too high — the invoice must be blocked or the amount corrected
VPMoving average varianceAfter posting, the moving average price is recalculated and the percentage deviation from the old price comparedBeyond it: moving average price variance exceeds tolerance
Tolerances are configured per company codeThe first key in OMR6 is the company code (because invoices are booked per company code). Different company codes in one group can therefore use different tolerances; on top of that a tolerance group can be assigned in the vendor master (one per vendor per company code), used to loosen or tighten the limits for specific vendors.

To post to a variance account by hand, enter the account number on the G/L account tab of MIRO. Note that the debit/credit direction is the opposite of a normal invoice: a normal invoice credits payables, and the direction of a variance line follows the increase or decrease of the invoice amount — get it wrong and the system reports that debits and credits do not balance.

9. Pass criteria and error comparison

  • MIRO posts 300 PC / 216,000.00 successfully and the status bar returns the invoice number; the Invoice line in the ME23N history carries it.
  • The balance for that PO in MB5S returns to 0.00.
  • Change the invoice amount to 226,800.00 and try again: M8083 is reported; saved as blocked, MIR4 shows the blocking reason.
  • After MRBR releases the blocked invoice, payables rise by 226,800.00 and the price variance account shows 10,800.00.
  • Remove GR-based IV in ME12, create a new PO and go straight to MIRO without a receipt: items appear and the invoice posts, while the PO history has no receipt.
  • Receive afterwards in MIGO: the MB5S balance returns to zero and both PO history lines are complete.

One sentence to remember how GR and IR relateGR increases stock and credits GR/IR; IR brings GR/IR back to zero and increases payables. Either can come first, but quantity and amount must agree on both sides; when they do not, the system does not "work it out" — it uses the tolerance to decide whether to pass the difference to a variance account or stop and ask a human.
