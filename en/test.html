A. Organisational structure: company code · plant · purchasing organization (10 questions)

  • Company code = the legal entity that reports externally; plant = where goods are received, stocked, planned and valuated; the storage location is only a position inside the plant.
  • The purchasing organization negotiates conditions with vendors; a plant can be served by several purchasing organizations but only one is flagged as the standard purchasing organization.
  • A plant belongs to exactly one company code; a purchasing organization can serve several plants across company codes.

Q1. What is the most important role of the company code (BUKRS) in MM?

The company code is the legal entity that produces the balance sheet and the profit and loss statement; chart of accounts, posting periods and currency all live at this level. The plant drives stock and valuation.

Q2. At which level is the stock quantity managed?

Stock is managed per material + plant + storage location; table MARD holds exactly that plant/storage-location stock. Neither the company code nor the purchasing organization holds quantities directly.

Q3. What is the purchasing organization responsible for?

The purchasing organization is the party that negotiates conditions; vendor purchasing data and purchasing document number ranges hang off it. Price valuation is a plant / company code matter.

Q4. How does the purchasing group (EKGRP) differ from the purchasing organization?

The purchasing group only answers "who looks after this order"; it never enters accounting. The purchasing organization is the party that negotiates conditions.

Q5. Can a plant be assigned to more than one purchasing organization?

A plant can be bought for by several purchasing organizations; "assign standard purchasing organization to plant" sets the default. Goods receipt without a PO that creates the order automatically depends on exactly this setting.

Q6. What does the valuation level decide?

The valuation level is a client-level setting and is normally "valuation at plant level" — which is why the same material can carry a different standard price in different plants.

Q7. Can a plant belong to two company codes?

Plant to company code is many-to-one: a plant has exactly one owner, while one company code can have several plants.

Q8. What is the relationship between purchasing organization and company code?

A purchasing organization can buy centrally across company codes, the usual group purchasing setup; the accounting still lands in each company code.

Q9. Does the storage location (LGORT) affect the purchase price?

The storage location only answers "where inside the plant the goods sit"; it affects neither valuation nor accounts.

Q10. Where does the MRP run take place?

MRP runs at plant level; the plant parameters (MRP group, planning horizon, goods receipt processing time) are maintained in the plant / plant parameters.

B. Material master: types · views · tables (12 questions)

  • The material type (ROH/FERT/HALB…) controls which views are available, the number range, whether valuation is mandatory and the field status.
  • MARA general data, MARC plant data, MARD storage location stock, MBEW valuation data; MARC and MARD are both keyed by plant.
  • The base unit of measure is used for stock management, the order unit for purchasing; the two may differ and the system converts between them.

Q1. Which of the following is NOT decided by the material type?

The vendor reconciliation account sits in the vendor master (company code level) and has nothing to do with the material type. The material type controls the views, the number range, the valuation control and the field selection.

Q2. What do ROH / HALB / FERT stand for?

ROH raw material, HALB semi-finished product, FERT finished product; HAWA is a trading good and DIEN a service.

Q3. Which table holds the material's general data (description, base unit, material group)?

MARA is the material general data table; MARC holds plant data, MARD storage location stock and MBEW valuation data.

Q4. Which table holds the plant-level MRP type and purchasing group?

MRP type, purchasing group and in-house production time all sit in MARC (plant level).

Q5. In which table is the unrestricted stock of one storage location recorded?

MARD holds stock per material + plant + storage location; MCHB is batch stock and MSKA sales order stock.

Q6. Which table holds the standard price, the moving average price and the valuation class?

MBEW is the material valuation table: per valuation area (usually the plant) it stores price control, standard price, moving average price and valuation class.

Q7. The material is missing the Purchasing view. What is the most direct consequence?

No purchasing view means the PO item cannot default the purchasing group, the order unit and so on; goods receipt and MRP look at the plant / MRP views instead.

Q8. The material has no Accounting view (it is not valuated). Which error is most likely?

Without valuation there is no stock account and no price, so the goods receipt cannot create an accounting document.

Q9. How do the base unit of measure and the order unit relate?

Stock is kept in the base unit while purchasing may use an order unit (box, pallet); the system converts through the unit conversion factors.

Q10. What is the difference between price control S and V?

S = standard price: the gap between the receipt price and the standard price goes to price difference. V = moving average: every receipt changes the stock unit price directly.

Q11. What is the difference between the deletion flag and deletion in the material master?

In practice you set the deletion flag to stop new documents referring to the material; physical deletion goes through archiving (MM74 and friends), never a single click.

Q12. The material group (MATKL) is closest to which role?

The material group feeds purchasing analysis and reporting plus part of account determination; it does not decide quantities or prices directly.

C. Vendor master and purchasing info records (10 questions)

  • In S/4HANA vendors are business partners: role FLVN00 (FI) + FLVN01 (MM), with the data spread over three levels: address / company code / purchasing organization.
  • The reconciliation account (for example 160000) sits at company code level; a wrong entry surfaces as failed account determination at invoice posting.
  • A purchasing info record = vendor + material + purchasing organization; the PO takes its net price from there first.

Q1. Which transaction / object is used to maintain a vendor in S/4HANA?

S/4 manages vendors and customers together as business partners; XK01 is obsolete and redirects to BP.

Q2. What does BP role FLVN01 stand for?

FLVN00 is the financial vendor and FLVN01 the purchasing vendor; the purchasing organization data hangs off FLVN01.

Q3. The vendor's "three levels of data" usually means?

The address level belongs to the BP itself; the company code level carries the reconciliation account and the payment terms; the purchasing organization level carries the order currency, the purchasing group and the two indicators.

Q4. A wrong reconciliation account on the vendor first shows up where?

The reconciliation account belongs to the company code level and relates to payables, so it usually surfaces as failed account determination when MIRO posts.

Q5. What is the key of a purchasing info record?

Info records are maintained per vendor / material / purchasing organization and can be refined further by plant.

Q6. When you create a PO, which source has top priority for the proposed net price?

The info record is read first (then contracts / scheduling agreements); manual entry only if the system allows it and nothing was found.

Q7. A PO reports "No net price could be determined". What is the most likely cause?

The net price comes from the info record; no record at all, or a price of zero, produces exactly that message.

Q8. Where can "GR-based IV" be changed?

Change it in the info record (that affects POs created afterwards); for an existing PO, untick it on the item's Invoice tab in ME22N.

Q9. What is the purpose of the source list?

The source list is the approved vendor list; goods receipt without a PO depends on it to find a source.

Q10. A vendor is blocked for purchasing. What is the most direct effect?

The purchasing block prevents new purchasing documents; existing documents and payment are governed by the central block / payment block instead.

D. Purchasing documents and process: requisition → order → framework agreement (12 questions)

  • A purchase requisition is internal demand and creates no accounting document; a purchase order is a commitment to the vendor and moves neither stock nor the books.
  • Convert requisitions with ME21N/ME57; order type NB standard, FO framework, UB stock transport.
  • Contracts and scheduling agreements are long-term agreements; RFQ, quotation and comparison are the ME41/ME47/ME49 set.

Q1. What is the essential difference between a purchase requisition and a purchase order?

A requisition only says what is wanted, how much and when; only the order commits externally, and neither of them creates an accounting document.

Q2. After saving a purchase order, what has changed in stock and in the books?

The PO is only a document: no logistics and no accounting. Only the 101 receipt increases stock and raises GR/IR.

Q3. Which transaction is most used to convert a requisition into an order?

In ME21N you enter the requisition number and press Enter to pull the items in; ME57 assigns and converts in bulk.

Q4. What do order types NB / FO / UB stand for?

NB standard, FO framework (related to contracts and scheduling agreements), UB stock transport order; returns normally use a returns order type such as RE.

Q5. Where is the release strategy on a purchase order used?

The release strategy implements the approval workflow: the order is created first, and until it is released it cannot be printed or received.

Q6. How does a contract differ from a scheduling agreement?

A contract fixes the total quantity and the price and you create call-off orders against it in ME21N; a scheduling agreement carries the delivery schedule itself (ME38/ME84).

Q7. Which transaction codes cover RFQ, quotation and price comparison?

ME41 request for quotation, ME47 maintain quotation, ME49 price comparison; ME31K creates a contract and ME31L a scheduling agreement.

Q8. How is the "still to be delivered" quantity on a purchase order calculated?

Still to be delivered = order quantity − quantity received; "still to be invoiced" is the gap between receipt and invoice.

Q9. In ME23N, which tab shows most directly where the order stands?

The purchase order history strings creation / goods receipt / invoice / payment into one timeline.

Q10. Number ranges for purchasing documents are usually assigned by?

Number ranges hang off the document type, so different order types can run on different ranges.

Q11. Can a purchase order reference a contract directly instead of a requisition?

Contracts and scheduling agreements are long-term agreements, so a call-off order can reference the contract directly; no prior requisition is needed.

Q12. In a returns scenario, how does a returns order differ from a normal order?

A returns order (order type RE, for example) sends goods back to the vendor; the return is posted with the reverse movement 122.

E. Goods receipt · stock movements · physical inventory (12 questions)

  • 101 receipt against an order, 122 return to vendor, 102 reversal of a receipt, 311 storage location transfer (same value, no accounting document), 301 plant-to-plant transfer (may create an accounting document).
  • 261 issue to production, 201 issue to a cost centre, 501 goods receipt without a purchase order, 561 initial stock load.
  • A stock change always comes with a material document; when value is involved an accounting document is created as well.

Q1. What does movement type 101 mean in business terms?

101 is "goods receipt to purchase order": stock increases and GR/IR is raised.

Q2. Which movement type sends received goods back to the vendor?

122 is the returns delivery (with reference to the order): stock decreases and GR/IR is reversed; 102 only reverses one receipt.

Q3. How do 102 and 122 differ?

102 reverses the same receipt document; 122 is a separate returns transaction that can reference the order.

Q4. Which movement type transfers stock between storage locations (0001 → 0002)?

311 creates a material document only, no accounting document (value-neutral transfer); 301 is the plant-to-plant transfer.

Q5. Which stock movement normally creates no accounting document?

311 moves stock at the same value inside one plant, so only a material document exists; cross-plant movements or a change in value do create an accounting document.

Q6. Which movement type issues components to a production order?

261 issues to an order or cost object; 201 issues to a cost centre.

Q7. Which movement type issues to a cost centre (not a production order)?

201 is the cost centre issue; the expense lands on the cost centre.

Q8. What is the core difference between 501 and 101?

501 is goods receipt without a purchase order: the value comes from the material price and goes to the configured account (usually consumption); no GR/IR is created.

Q9. What is the correct use of 561?

561 is used for the initial data load and usually pairs with an opening balance account.

Q10. What does posting a physical inventory difference (MI07) produce?

The difference adjusts stock and profit and loss by value, so both a material document and an accounting document are created.

Q11. Which documents does a single 101 receipt normally create?

The receipt updates both the stock and the GR/IR accrual, which is why both documents exist.

Q12. What is the default behaviour when the receipt quantity exceeds the order quantity?

The receipt tolerance (IMG: tolerance limits for goods receipt) permits a certain overdelivery percentage by default; beyond it the posting errors.

F. Invoice verification and GR/IR (12 questions)

  • Three-way match: purchase order (what we owe), goods receipt (what arrived), invoice (what is being charged).
  • Tolerance keys in OMR6: BD small differences, DQ quantity deviation, PP price change, VP moving average price difference.
  • An invoice beyond tolerance can still be posted but payment is blocked; release it with MRBR. The only exits from GR/IR are an invoice or MR11.

Q1. What does invoice verification (MIRO) do?

MIRO matches the three documents and turns the GR/IR accrual into payables; the payment itself is F-53/F110.

Q2. When is the GR/IR accrual account cleared?

The receipt posts to the credit side of GR/IR and the invoice posting debits it back; payment clears payables.

Q3. What does tolerance key BD in OMR6 mean?

BD = form small differences; DQ quantity, PP price, VP moving average price.

Q4. The invoice amount is 5% above the order net price. What is the most likely handling?

Exceeding the PP tolerance triggers the price check (typically message M8083); the invoice is blocked and only an MRBR release allows payment.

Q5. Which transaction releases a blocked invoice?

MRBR releases blocked invoices; MIR4 displays, MIR7 parks and MR8M reverses.

Q6. How does a parked invoice (MIR7) differ from MIRO?

MIR7 saves a parked document; posting still happens through MIRO or a later step.

Q7. Which transaction reverses a posted invoice?

MR8M reverses the invoice document; only then can you re-enter it or return the goods.

Q8. A credit balance in GR/IR usually means?

The receipt posted to the credit side with no invoice yet gives a credit balance; if the invoice came first the balance is a debit.

Q9. What do invoice document types RE and RN stand for?

RE is the (gross) invoice and RN the credit memo; negative amounts use the credit memo.

Q10. With "GR-based IV" unticked, you may?

Unticked, the invoice is proposed from the ordered quantity, so invoice-before-goods works; ticked, a goods receipt must exist.

Q11. What is a subsequent debit used for?

A subsequent debit adds payables after the original invoice; a subsequent credit is the opposite.

Q12. One invoice covers both a delivery and a return. What is the correct approach?

One invoice may mix debit and credit items; adjust the direction with the button next to the purchase order reference where necessary.

G. Valuation and automatic account determination (OBYC) (10 questions)

  • Standard price (S): the price is fixed and differences go to a price difference account; moving average price (V): the unit price is recalculated on every receipt.
  • Automatic account determination finds the account by transaction key + valuation class (+ valuation grouping code): BSX stock, WRX GR/IR, PRD price difference, GBB goods issue and consumption.
  • The valuation class comes from the material's accounting view and selects the account group; the valuation grouping code groups plants.

Q1. With price control S, where does the difference between the receipt price and the standard price go?

A standard price material is received at standard, and the difference goes to price difference; that is what keeps standard cost stable.

Q2. With price control V, invoice differences usually?

Under moving average the difference goes into the stock value and the unit price moves with it.

Q3. Which account does transaction key WRX point to?

WRX = GR/IR clearing; BSX = stock, PRD = price difference, GBB = goods issue and consumption.

Q4. What does transaction key BSX determine?

BSX gives the inventory account by valuation class; different valuation classes can point to different stock accounts.

Q5. A goods receipt reports "Account determination failed". What is the most likely cause?

Account determination = transaction key + valuation class (+ valuation grouping code) with no matching entry in OBYC.

Q6. Where is the valuation class (BKLAS) maintained?

The valuation class is entered in the material's accounting view and selects the set of automatic account determination accounts.

Q7. What does the valuation grouping code (KALK in OBYC) mainly solve?

The valuation grouping code lets you split accounts by plant (or by group); the same valuation class can then post to different account groups.

Q8. Why is a standard price a poor fit for raw materials whose price moves often?

A standard price freezes the price, so market swings all land in price difference; whether that suits you is a management decision.

Q9. OBYC configuration belongs to which kind of work?

OBYC is automatic account determination customising: change it once and every related posting is affected.

Q10. Can one material use S in plant A and V in plant B?

Price control sits at valuation area level; with plant-level valuation each plant may use S or V.

H. MRP and requirements planning (10 questions)

  • Net requirement = demand + safety stock − available stock − already ordered (in transit).
  • MRP type PD plans by demand, ND takes no part, VB is manual reorder point; lot size EX exact, FX fixed, HB replenish up to the maximum.
  • An MRP run creates purchase requisitions (external) or planned orders (in-house); MD04 shows the document chain.

Q1. What is the basic net requirement formula?

Net requirements bring demand, safety stock, current stock and open orders into one calculation.

Q2. How do MRP types PD and ND differ?

PD is normal MRP; ND is excluded from the run entirely (used for manually controlled materials).

Q3. What do lot-sizing procedures EX / FX / HB mean?

EX orders exactly the net requirement, FX a fixed quantity and HB tops the stock up to the maximum stock level.

Q4. After an MRP run, externally procured materials usually produce?

MRP creates purchase requisitions or planned orders; converting to a PO is the external commitment (direct PO creation can be configured).

Q5. What can you see in MD04?

MD04 is the stock/requirements list: every planning-relevant element in one table.

Q6. What is safety stock for?

Safety stock enters the net requirement formula as a buffer quantity, not as an order.

Q7. What does the planning time fence control?

The fence protects near-term plans from automatic MRP changes and avoids constant rescheduling.

Q8. What is an MRP area used for?

An MRP area can be a storage location or a sub-plant, giving a finer planning granularity.

Q9. What typically distinguishes strategy group 10 from 40?

Strategy 10 is MTS (make-to-stock); 40 is MTO, where the sales order creates the requirement directly.

Q10. In an MRP run, what does "already ordered" mean?

Open order quantities count as supply; otherwise the system would buy the same material twice.

I. Special procurement: consignment · subcontracting · transfers · framework agreements (8 questions)

  • Consignment: the goods sit in your plant but belong to the vendor; settlement only happens on withdrawal (movement types in the 411 K family).
  • Subcontracting: you send components to the vendor and the components are backflushed from the BOM at goods receipt.
  • Transfers: the stock transport order (UB) is for cross-plant moves; framework agreements come as contract and scheduling agreement.

Q1. What characterises consignment stock?

Consignment stock is physically in your plant but on the vendor's books; you withdraw it with movement types in the 411/431 family and create payables at that point.

Q2. What does the system do with the components at a subcontracting goods receipt?

The subcontract PO carries the component list; at the 101 receipt the components are consumed per the BOM and the finished product is taken into stock.

Q3. What is a statistical material normally used for?

A statistical material records quantities for analysis only and carries no inventory value; pipeline material is the classic case.

Q4. How does a stock transport order (UB) differ from a transfer posting (301)?

UB is for cross-plant transfers that need a formal document (shipping and scheduling included); 301 posts directly.

Q5. Which two main forms of framework agreement exist?

Contract (ME31K) and scheduling agreement (ME31L), followed by call-off orders or delivery schedules respectively.

Q6. How does batch management differ from serial number management?

A batch number traces a lot (shelf life, quality); a serial number uniquely identifies one piece.

Q7. What characterises third-party procurement?

In third-party business the physical flow bypasses your warehouse; only the document flow and the settlement flow pass through you.

Q8. Which movement types are normally used to withdraw consignment stock?

Consignment withdrawal uses 411 K (from consignment stock to own stock or consumption), unlike a normal 101.

J. Reports · transaction codes · authorisations and periods (10 questions)

  • Documents with MB51, stock with MB52/MMBE, GR/IR with MB5S, material list with MM60, purchase orders with ME2L/ME2M.
  • For authorisation errors use SU53 to see the missing object; personal defaults are SU3 (LANG/WRK/BUKRS).
  • Periods: accounting period OB52, material period MMRV/MMPV; both must be open before you can receive.

Q1. Which transaction lists material documents by selection criteria?

MB51 is the material document list (by material / movement type / period); MB52 is the stock list.

Q2. What is the easiest way to see a material's stock in each storage location?

MMBE gives the single-material overview, MB52 the mass stock list.

Q3. Which transaction reconciles the GR/IR balance at month-end?

MB5S lists receipts, invoices and balances per purchase order item and is the first choice for GR/IR reconciliation.

Q4. Which transaction summarises purchase orders by material?

ME2M lists purchase orders by material and ME2L by vendor.

Q5. To list all materials of one material type, use?

MM60 is the material list report (filtered by type and plant).

Q6. What is the easiest transaction for looking directly at MARA/MARC data?

SE16N is the table browser; SM30 maintains customising views and ST05 is the SQL trace.

Q7. You get "You are not authorised to execute transaction X". What do you check first?

SU53 shows the object of the last failed authorisation check; hand it to Basis or the security consultant to add the role.

Q8. What does SU3 do?

SU3 only affects your personal defaults; it changes neither the system configuration nor anyone else's settings.

Q9. A month-end goods receipt reports "Posting period is not open". What do you check?

A goods receipt writes stock and accounting, so both periods must include the current month.

Q10. What is the proper way to find out what triggers a status bar message and how to handle it?

Searching by message number (M8083, M7021) shows the trigger conditions and the recommended handling.

K. Consolidated mock exam (20 questions)

The questions below are no longer grouped by chapter; all of them are the "what would you do on the spot" kind: where do you look first when the error appears, which two lines moved in the books, does this step create a document.

Q1. You created a material but cannot select it in a purchase order. Which view is most likely missing?

The PO item takes the purchasing group, the order unit and similar data from the purchasing view; without that view the material cannot be selected or defaulted.

Q2. Goods receipt says "Material is not maintained in plant 1710". What does that mean?

Without the plant view (MARC) the system treats the material as non-existent in that plant.

Q3. After receiving 300 PC against order 4500000123, an invoice for 300 PC arrives. What is the GR/IR balance now?

The receipt posts to the credit side and the invoice debits it back; equal amounts net to zero.

Q4. The invoice amount is 0.5% above the receipt and sits inside the default tolerance. What is the result?

Within tolerance the posting is allowed and the difference goes to the difference account per the BD/PP rules; payment is not blocked.

Q5. The purchase order is fully invoiced and you need one more invoice. What should you do?

Once the invoiced quantity is used up no further invoice is possible; the original invoice has to be reversed first.

Q6. You want to turn "receive first, invoice later" into "invoice first, receive later". What do you change?

With the indicator removed MIRO can invoice the ordered quantity; existing POs need to be changed one by one in ME22N.

Q7. Which of these is NOT needed for a goods receipt without PO to create the order automatically?

The automatic creation works from the source list and the info record; a prior requisition is not required.

Q8. What is the accounting effect of a standard storage location transfer (311)?

Same plant, same value, so the value does not change and no accounting document is produced.

Q9. What is the usual journal entry for a production issue (261)?

The issue moves material cost into the production order or cost object and reduces stock.

Q10. A standard price material is received above the standard price. Where does the difference go?

The standard price fixes the value taken into stock, so the difference goes to the price difference account.

Q11. Which of the following is not a purchasing document?

A material document belongs to inventory management; requisition, order and contract are purchasing documents.

Q12. The purchase requisition created by an MRP run carries which number range?

The number range assigned to the document type decides the range.

Q13. Safety stock rises from 20 to 120 with everything else unchanged. What happens to the MRP net requirement?

Net requirement = demand + safety stock − stock − open orders, so more safety stock raises the net requirement directly.

Q14. The vendor has no data for purchasing organization 1710. What appears when you create a PO?

The purchasing organization level of the vendor is missing, so the PO cannot default the order currency or the payment terms.

Q15. Which transaction shows the change history of a material?

MM04 shows the change documents of the material master: who changed which field and when.

Q16. An invoice is blocked. What are you most likely to see when paying?

The block stops payment; only after the MRBR release can the item be cleared.

Q17. At month-end GR/IR still shows a credit balance. What does that mean?

A credit balance means received but not invoiced; follow up the invoice at month-end or handle it with MR11.

Q18. To restrict a buyer to orders of their own purchasing group, what do you use?

Authorisations restrict by fields such as the purchasing group; that is role design.

Q19. Which of these affects both the stock quantity and the accounting value?

101 increases stock and raises GR/IR; 311 only moves the stock location; saving a PO touches no books.

Q20. The same material uses standard price in plant 1710 and moving average price in plant 1720. Is that possible?

With plant-level valuation the price control is maintained plant by plant.
