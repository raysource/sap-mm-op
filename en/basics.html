MM (Materials Management) is the part of SAP that answers: what do we buy, how much, when does it arrive, where is it booked, and how is the money recorded. It connects the purchasing process (requisition, order, goods receipt) on one side with Financial Accounting (stock, accruals, payables) on the other, and the material master plus the organisational structure hold the two together.

5. Movement types: the coding of every stock action

A movement type decides three things: whether stock goes up or down, whether an accounting document is created, and which offsetting account is used.

261Goods issue to production order−Consumption Dr / stock CrIssuing material to the line

101 versus 501Both increase stock; the difference is whether there is a purchase order. 101 requires a PO, values the receipt at the order's net price and posts to GR/IR. 501 has no PO, values the receipt at the material's standard price and posts straight to a consumption (expense) account. So 501 never creates a GR/IR balance and can never be matched in invoice verification — such deliveries are either expensed or covered by a PO created afterwards and reversed.

6. Accounting: why a GR/IR account exists at all

In reality goods and invoices never arrive at the same moment. SAP uses the GR/IR (Goods Receipt / Invoice Receipt) account to record that gap: at goods receipt it recognises a liability for goods received but not yet invoiced, and when the invoice arrives it converts that accrual into real payables.

Three rules of thumb① Goods received but not invoiced → GR/IR shows a credit balance (an accrual liability). ② Invoiced before receipt (requires GR-based IV to be switched off) → GR/IR shows a debit balance (a prepayment). ③ At month-end, use MB5S to inspect GR/IR items that never clear, and MR11 to reclassify them if needed.

7. Automatic account determination (OBYC): nobody keys the accounts

The accounts on stock-related documents are derived by the system from transaction key + valuation class + account modifier, configured in OBYC. That is why valuation class 3000 in the material master is not a comment field — it decides which inventory account the goods land in.

The fastest way to read a documentAfter any posting, note the accounting document number and open it in FB03: the line items show the posting key (PK — 40 = debit, 50 = credit), the account, the debit/credit indicator and the amount. The single biggest step for a beginner is going from "I clicked something" to "these lines appeared in the books".

8. Customising: the ten items to walk through before a new system goes live

Business users never touch configuration, yet eight times out of ten "why won't my document save" comes down to something missing here. The ten items below are in go-live order: the first four are organisational, the middle four cover planning and goods receipt, and the last two are the accounting interface. Each one names the IMG path and who you should ask.

Configuration versus master dataConfiguration (IMG) is the rule set, owned by consultants and key users, and one change affects the whole company; master data (MM01/BP/ME11) is the data business users maintain every day. Treat a configuration problem as master data and you will trial-and-error in MM01; treat master data as configuration and you will change something for the whole company. The test: does a different material or vendor fail the same way — if yes, check configuration; if only this one fails, check master data.

9. Common misconceptions (clear these before going further)

  • "Posting a purchase order reserves stock" — no. A PO moves no stock; only a goods receipt (101) does.
  • "Invoice verification just means typing the invoice in" — no. It performs a three-way match (order / goods receipt / invoice); quantities and amounts must agree and variances go through tolerance.
  • "Stock value = quantity × order price" — true only for standard-price materials whose invoice price did not change. A moving-average material recalculates its unit price on every receipt.
  • "Transfers affect profit" — a 311 transfer inside one plant moves no value and creates no accounting document; only a cross-valuation-area transfer (301) at a different price produces a variance account.
  • "Deleting a goods receipt makes it disappear" — you cannot delete it, only reverse it (102 or 122); and if it has been invoiced you must reverse the invoice first (MR8M).
